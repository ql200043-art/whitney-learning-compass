# Real-user impact study — ready to administer, not yet performed

Prepared October 23, 2026. **Participant observations, task times, ratings and quotations are uncollected.** Technical evaluations and automated browser walkthroughs are supplementary evidence, not a substitute for real-user impact.

## Context and consent

The project author is a Yale MBA/MEM joint-degree student assigned to Eli Whitney Museum & Workshop through the Yale SOM Nonprofit Board Fellows program. That experience inspired this independently conceived course prototype. No museum commission, use, approval or evaluation has been established.

Recruit one K–12 teacher if possible; otherwise another educator or program practitioner, or an explicitly identified graduate-student proxy. Record role and relevant experience, not name, email, school or student records. A proxy's ratings cannot establish classroom usefulness for teachers. No participant has been contacted by the assistant.

Read: “This is an independent course prototype, not an official museum service. Participation is voluntary and you may stop. We will record task timings and brief observations without student information. May I take anonymous notes? May I quote your feedback anonymously in the course report? Audio/video is optional and requires separate permission.” Record each permission separately. Do not record or quote without consent; honor withdrawal.

## 10–15-minute session

1. Consent, role, prior website/app familiarity, one-minute neutral introduction (2 minutes).
2. Two comparable **program-selection tasks**, up to 3 minutes each. Start the timer when the task is shown, stop when the participant identifies a program and states grade, subtotal, duration and uncertainty. Include corrections, record assistance and timeouts. Keep the device/network conditions comparable.
3. In the app, explicitly select a valid returned program and inspect/edit its three-phase plan (up to 3 minutes). Measure this separately; the museum website is not described as a lesson generator. If a program-selection task is incomplete, record that and separately demonstrate a labeled valid scenario without counting it as independent completion.
4. Ratings and open feedback (2–4 minutes). Allow negative feedback and report failures.

For one participant, randomize both tool order and which task is assigned to each tool by coin toss. With more participants, counterbalance. Do not ask the same task twice without acknowledging recall. Two tasks cannot be perfectly equivalent; state topic familiarity, task assignment and order as limitations. The short cap may disadvantage unfamiliar tools; timeouts are censored observations, not completion times.

### Selection tasks and independently checkable answer keys

**Task X:** Grade 4, 24 students, force/motion/engineering design, $400 program-fee budget (disclosed USD interpretation), maximum 90 minutes. Identify one suitable option and explain a remaining museum question. Catapult is a plausible target: published Grade 4, $14 × 24 = $336, 90 minutes. Marble Roller Coaster and Simple Machines also satisfy these constraints; educational relevance requires a reason. Bridges and Rubber Band Car are not verified within the time limit because their durations are unpublished.

**Task Y:** Grade 5, 24 students, renewable energy and engineering design, identical budget and time. Wind Power is a plausible target: published Grade 5, $15 × 24 = $360, 90 minutes. Solar Sunflower is another relevant option at $14 × 24 = $336 and 90 minutes. Do not require one exact program when another supported choice is appropriate.

Answer-key authority: [curated records and field evidence](../data/programs.json), [matching rules](matching_rules.md); [Catapult](https://www.eliwhitney.org/catapult), [Wind Power](https://www.eliwhitney.org/sustainable-energy-wind-power), [Solar Sunflower](https://www.eliwhitney.org/solar-sunflower). Published details are historical catalog facts; verify any apparent current-page difference and annotate the task before judging the participant. Fees exclude transport, meals and unknown extra charges; availability/capacity/accessibility remain unconfirmed.

### Equivalent outcome checklist

- Genuine program with correct official source.
- All requested grades supported.
- Correct per-student multiplication and budget comparison, with USD assumption disclosed.
- Published duration within limit, or correctly identified as unresolved (unresolved is not a confirmed suitable choice).
- No invented standards, booking confirmation or accommodation claim.
- Educational rationale connects the program to the task.

Score each item independently. Report task completion and correctness together; a fast but invalid choice is not a successful time saving. Additional journey review uses the same 1–5 usefulness anchors in [methodology](evaluation_methodology.md), records needed edits, and checks predictions versus actual evidence. No observed student-learning outcome is inferred.
