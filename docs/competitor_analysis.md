# Positioning and traditional-workflow baseline

Official pages directly reviewed October 23, 2026: [museum catalog](https://www.eliwhitney.org/workshop/school-programs), [MagicSchool lesson planner](https://www.magicschool.ai/tools/lesson-plan), [TripSlip](https://trytripslip.com/). These are public-page capability observations, not hands-on testing of paid competitor accounts or a comprehensive market search. Vendor benefit claims are not independently verified impact evidence for this project.

The museum catalog visibly offers keyword/title search, grade, subject and Next Gen Standard selectors with Apply, program links and published grade labels, and a Book a Program link. It is a substantial educational catalog, not merely a list without filters. Its site serves the museum's authoritative information and booking process. The prototype's 20 curated records are narrower than the official catalog and may become stale.

MagicSchool describes generating structured lesson drafts from grade, subject, standards, goals and time, with teacher refinement. TripSlip describes venue discovery/booking, availability, parent communications, permission forms, payments and coordination tracking. These are relevant overlapping categories. This review does not establish either product's Eli Whitney-specific catalog validation, evidence-pointer system or exact curriculum-to-workshop workflow. It does not claim those features are absent.

## Fair workflow comparison — not a timed study

| Planning activity | Traditional workflow | Prototype workflow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find programs | Apply museum filters; read detail pages | Submit goals/constraints; inspect Advisor candidates |
| Check constraints | Teacher checks every grade, multiplies fee and checks time across options | Existing deterministic engine computes checks and retains unknowns |
| Assess educational fit | Teacher interprets descriptions against objectives | Teacher reviews generated reasoning and compares authoritative facts |
| Prepare instruction | Teacher independently creates classroom preparation/reflection | Teacher selects, receives suggestions, edits and reviews three phases |
| Establish booking readiness | Contact museum through official process | Still contact museum; inquiry draft is assistance, not confirmation |

No task-time saving has been measured. Additional planning output is evaluated separately from common program-selection work. The app introduces its own costs: model latency, source staleness, generated-text review and possible provider failure.

The defensible differentiation hypothesis is **evidence-grounded curriculum-to-workshop planning that integrates deterministic eligibility, two specialized agents, teacher selection, instructional editing and source review in one museum-specific workflow**. It is not a claim of being first, unique worldwide, endorsed or superior. The [real-user protocol](impact_study_protocol.md) is needed to test whether this integration actually helps a prospective user.
